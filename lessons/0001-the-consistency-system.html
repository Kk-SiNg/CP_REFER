Why Consistency Beats Volume

There's a concept in cognitive science called the spacing effect (Wikipedia): practice distributed over time produces stronger, longer-lasting memories than the same amount of practice crammed into one session.

⚡ The Math

Solving 3 problems per day for 90 consecutive days = 270 problems.
Solving 8 problems per day for 10 days, then 0 for 10 days, repeated = 270 problems.

Same volume. Radically different outcomes. The first builds compound pattern recognition. The second builds and demolishes it in cycles.

Look at your heatmap. The green blocks come in clusters. Dec-Jan is dead. Then Feb-Apr is intense. May drops. Jun-Jul has gaps. Every restart costs you 3-5 days of "warming back up" — that's wasted practice.

The System: 30-Day Chain

Here's a concrete, no-motivation-required system. The goal is simple: solve at least one CF problem every single day for 30 days straight. Not 5 problems. Not a whole contest. Just one.

1

Set the floor absurdly low

Your minimum daily commitment is 1 problem. Any rating. Even an 800-rated problem counts. The point is to never have a zero day. On good days, you'll naturally do 3-5+. On bad days, you still do 1.

2

Pick a fixed time slot

Tie it to something you already do. "After lunch" or "after dinner" — not "when I feel like it." Motivation is unreliable. Time slots are not. You said you have 2+ hours. Block 2 hours at the same time every day.

3

Use a daily practice template

Every session follows this structure. No deciding what to do — just execute:

📋 Daily Session Template (2 hours)

BlockTimeWhat
Warmup 10 min Solve 1 problem at your comfort rating (1200-1300)
Push 60 min Attempt 1-2 problems at your stretch rating (1400-1500)
Upsolve 30 min Read editorial for problems you couldn't solve, implement the solution
Log 5 min Write 2-3 bullet points: what you learned, what tripped you up
4

Track the chain visually

Your CF heatmap is already a chain tracker. Make it your scoreboard. Open your profile every day and look at the green. The chain becomes something you don't want to break. Jerry Seinfeld used this exact method to write jokes — "Don't break the chain."

5

Plan for bad days in advance

You will have days where you're exhausted, have exams, or just don't feel like it. Those are "800-rated problem" days. Have a mental list of easy problems ready. The rule is: never zero, even if it's tiny.

What Changes at 30 Days

Here's what happens when your max streak goes from 15 → 30:

✓ Pattern recognition compounds

After 30 consecutive days of seeing problems, your brain starts recognizing problem "shapes" — that "this feels like a DSU problem" or "this has a binary search on answer structure." This intuition cannot be built in bursts.

✓ Contest performance stabilizes

Your rating graph oscillates between 1020 and 1195. That's a 175-point swing. Consistent practice narrows this band. You stop having "cold" contests because you're never cold.

✓ The restart tax disappears

Every break costs you ~3-5 days of "remembering where I was." Over 3 months with 4-5 breaks, that's 15-25 days of wasted practice. That's an entire month of rating-building work, gone.
